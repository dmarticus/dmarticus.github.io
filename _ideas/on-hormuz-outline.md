# On Hormuz — distilled outline

## Core claim

- Geography is reasserting itself over financial and technological abstraction
- The post-Cold War assumption
  - open seas and US naval dominance reduce the importance of location
  - participation in global trade can outweigh physical coordinates
- Hormuz exposes the limit of that assumption
  - Iran converted control of a chokepoint into direct negotiating power
  - states and firms are adapting independently of Washington
- The same lens clarifies
  - trade baskets
  - proxy conflict
  - sanctions
  - energy sovereignty
  - changing European trade routes
  - physical constraints beneath AI

## Entry point

- Citrini Research field report
  - analyst observed tanker traffic near Oman
  - direct conversations with fishermen and shipping captains
  - evidence of Iran’s checkpoint process
- Conversation with Cameron
  - sharpened the geography-first interpretation
  - expanded the frame beyond oil prices
- Intellectual precedent
  - Robert Kaplan’s geographic thesis
  - Peter Zeihan’s geographic and demographic thesis
- Novelty claimed
  - not geography as a new theory
  - geography being operationalized visibly and in real time

## What the trade baskets reveal

- Trade basket
  - group of securities expressing one causal thesis
  - allocation weights encode conviction or exposure
- Three baskets map three transmission mechanisms
  - immediate shipping scarcity
  - commodity-price disruption
  - durable political demand for domestic energy

### Tanker basket

- Holdings
  - tanker operators
  - freight-rate exposure through BWET
- Mechanism
  1. Shipping lanes become dangerous
  2. Vessels reroute around Africa
  3. Voyage duration increases
  4. Effective ship supply tightens
  5. Freight rates rise
- Evidence
  - BWET had quadrupled since Citrini’s recommendation

### Energy and commodities basket

- Exposure
  - drilling
  - refining
  - pipelines
  - fertilizer
  - petrochemicals
- Mechanism
  - disruption raises energy prices
  - natural-gas costs transmit into fertilizer
- Evidence
  - oil moved from $67 to roughly $118 at the peak
  - Gulf infrastructure expected to require 250–275 days of repair
- Highest-conviction angle
  - US petrochemical producers
  - cheap domestic feedstock
  - benefit can persist beyond reopening Hormuz

### Energy sovereignty basket

- Exposure
  - European and Japanese renewables
  - grid infrastructure
  - solar hardware
  - utilities
- Mechanism
  - visible vulnerability creates political permission for domestic energy buildout
  - governments accelerate permits, subsidies, and grid investment
- Utility economics
  - near-zero marginal-cost renewable assets
  - wholesale prices set by gas
  - gas spikes expand margins without equivalent cost increases
- Time horizon
  - slower and less volatile than disruption trades
  - potentially the most durable thesis
- Near-term contradiction
  - ceasefire news hurt all three baskets
  - structural logic may outlast short-term repricing

## Hormuz as a literal toll booth

- Iran did not fully close the strait
- Checkpoint system through the Qeshm–Larak channel
  - owners submit vessel, flag, cargo, and crew details
  - payment through cash, crypto, or diplomatic arrangements
  - approved ships receive escorts through Iranian waters
- Observed traffic
  - at least 15 ships on April 2
  - 2–5 daily in earlier weeks
  - multiple Asian, European, and regional flags
- Strategic facts
  - narrowest point is 21 miles wide
  - roughly 20% of global oil passes through
  - Iran controls the northern shore
- Implication
  - physical leverage cannot be sanctioned away
  - countries negotiate access individually
  - transit outside Washington’s approval signals erosion of the old order

## Proxy-war frame

- Cameron’s interpretation
  - Iran and Ukraine resemble pre-world-war proxy contests
  - major powers test a new order without direct conflict
- Mediation pattern
  - Pakistan’s role in US–Iran talks
  - interpreted through Chinese influence
- Status
  - useful framing hypothesis
  - not established as the post’s primary evidentiary claim

## Iran: geography creates options

- Possible sanctions-relief effect
  - 1.5–2 million barrels per day could return to normal markets
- Existing constraint
  - discounted sales to China and India
  - shadow fleets
  - limited buyer set
- Sanctions-relief mechanism
  - restored banking access
  - normal pricing
  - wider trade relationships
- Strategic shift
  - Hormuz provides bargaining leverage
  - SWIFT access could reduce dependence on Beijing
- Tension
  - China benefits from Iran’s isolation
  - Iranian leverage may create room to diversify away from China

## Cuba: location survives economic collapse

- Geographic role
  - flanks the Yucatan Channel
  - flanks the Straits of Florida
  - sits beside shipping routes carrying Gulf oil production
- Historical frame
  - Mahan’s “key to the Gulf of Mexico” analogy
- Current external presence
  - Russian warships with hypersonic missiles in Havana
  - formal Russia–Cuba military cooperation
  - four identified Chinese signals-intelligence sites
  - Russian tanker breaking the US fuel blockade
- Domestic conditions
  - maximum US pressure
  - population decline through emigration
  - power-grid collapse
  - weak near-term case for tourism or nearshoring
- Core implication
  - economic weakness does not erase chokepoint value
  - Russia and China already treat Cuba as strategically important
- Author’s strategic claim
  - Cuba constrains Gulf access for the US
  - US pressure can also limit Russian reach in the Western Hemisphere

## The EU: ambition constrained by geography

- Potential upside
  - common defense spending
  - joint industrial policy
  - shared debt issuance
  - new European investment surface
- Why the scenario is underpriced
  - potentially transformative
  - institutionally difficult
- Franco-German fracture
  - FCAS conflict over workshare
  - disagreement over common EU debt
  - competing industrial-policy blocs
- Energy transition progress
  - EUR 300 billion in REPowerEU
  - Russian gas share cut from 45% to 13%
  - US LNG imports tripled
  - wind and solar surpassed fossil generation in 2025
- Residual vulnerability
  - Hormuz directly affects only about 10% of EU LNG
  - Dutch gas benchmarks still nearly doubled
  - Slovenia introduced fuel rationing
  - ECB postponed rate cuts
- Implication
  - investment and diversification reduce exposure
  - they do not dissolve physical dependence

## Arctic routes and Europe’s internal geography

- Historical pattern
  - European power and trade flow east–west through the Mediterranean
  - Spain, Italy, and Greece gain strategic weight from sea lanes
- Emerging northern route
  - Chinese Arctic Express links Shanghai with northern European ports
  - reported transit time of 18 days
  - Suez comparison of 35–50 days
  - first ice-free Arctic day projected before 2030
- Potential winners
  - United Kingdom
  - Netherlands
  - Norway
- Potential loser
  - Mediterranean strategic centrality
- Italian response
  - investment in Trieste as a rail gateway to Central Europe
- Political consequence
  - Europe is pulled toward northern and southern trade geographies at once
  - climate change may make EU consolidation harder
- Degree of certainty
  - route shift is emerging rather than settled
  - full Arctic thesis remains speculative and incomplete

## Bits still depend on atoms

- Popular AI abstraction
  - software is weightless
  - cloud resources are location-independent
  - data moves without meaningful geography
- Physical substrate
  - roughly 50 gigawatts of hyperscaler compute capacity planned in one year
  - approximately $600 billion in capital expenditure
  - every gigawatt requires power in a specific place
  - hyperscalers are acquiring energy assets and reserving turbines and supply
- Scaling constraint
  - where power can physically be connected
- Concentrated supply chain
  - ASML produces roughly 70 EUV tools annually
  - estimated 3.5 EUV tools per gigawatt of AI compute
  - possible ceiling near 200 gigawatts by 2030 if execution succeeds
  - TSMC fabricates leading chips in Taiwan
  - Samsung and SK Hynix supply high-bandwidth memory from South Korea
- Strategic implication
  - AI expansion increases dependence on a few facilities and countries
  - several critical nodes sit near Pacific great-power competition
  - more compute deepens geographic exposure rather than eliminating it

## Emerging working model

### Read geopolitical events through physical constraints

1. Identify the chokepoint or scarce facility
2. Map who controls access
3. Trace which actors can reroute and at what cost
4. Observe direct negotiation outside legacy institutions
5. Translate the constraint into market, diplomatic, and infrastructure responses

### Distinguish time horizons

- Immediate
  - freight scarcity
  - oil and gas price shocks
  - ceasefire repricing
- Medium term
  - repair cycles
  - sanctions relief
  - energy rationing
  - altered trade relationships
- Long term
  - energy sovereignty
  - Arctic routes
  - EU cohesion
  - AI power and semiconductor concentration

### Look for durable advantage

- fixed geographic position
- control of narrow transit routes
- domestic energy capacity
- access to power, fabrication, memory, and lithography
- ability to create alternative routes or supplier relationships

## Tensions worth preserving

- A ceasefire weakens short-term trades without removing structural vulnerability
- Iran’s isolation strengthens China, while sanctions relief could widen Iran’s options
- Cuba can be economically broken and strategically valuable at the same time
- Europe has materially diversified energy and remains vulnerable to distant disruption
- Arctic access may improve trade efficiency while deepening political division inside Europe
- US naval hegemony remains relevant while individual actors increasingly route around it
- AI appears geographically abstract while becoming more dependent on physical concentration
- Climate change damages existing systems while creating new strategic routes
- The EU-superpower case may be underpriced because it is both consequential and genuinely difficult

## Likely conclusion

- Geography was never superseded by markets or software
- Hormuz makes physical leverage visible through an operating checkpoint system
- Energy, shipping, diplomacy, and compute all depend on fixed places
- Actors that recognize and exploit those constraints will shape the next decade
- AI intensifies the importance of geography because its scale requires concentrated power and hardware
